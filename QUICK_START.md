# 🎯 DebateChecker – Quick Start Guide

## 5 Simple Steps to Use DebateChecker

### Step 1: Open the App
Just visit the website. No login needed, no registration, nothing.

### Step 2: Write Your Argument
Paste or type any claim, opinion, or statement you want to test:
- "Everyone who exercises lives past 100"
- "AI will replace all jobs"
- "Social media makes people depressed"
- Any debate point you're making

### Step 3: Click "Analyze Argument"
One button. That's it. The app instantly analyzes your text.

### Step 4: Read the Results
You get 6 instant reports:
- ❌ Logical fallacies (errors in your thinking)
- 📊 How strong your evidence is
- ⚔️ What others might say against you
- 🔍 Hidden assumptions you made
- 💡 How to improve your argument
- 📈 Overall strength score (0-100%)

### Step 5: Improve & Try Again
Use the feedback to make your argument stronger, then submit again.

## Real-World Examples

### Example 1: Student Writing an Essay

**Your Claim:**
"Video games make kids violent because I play games and I'm aggressive"

**DebateChecker Finds:**
- ❌ Hasty Generalization (one person ≠ all people)
- ⚔️ Counter: "Most gamers aren't violent"
- 💡 Add: "Research shows..." or "Studies prove..."
- Score: 35% → Very Weak

**After Improvement:**
"Research indicates that while some studies show a correlation between violent games and aggression, the relationship is complex and depends on many factors"

**New Score:** 72% → Moderate ✅

## 7 Most Common Problems It Finds

| Problem | What It Means | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| **Hasty Generalization** | One example doesn't mean all | "I know someone who smokes and is healthy, so smoking is safe" |
| **Ad Hominem** | Attacking person, not argument | "You're stupid, so your opinion is wrong" |
| **False Cause** | A happened after B, so B caused A | "I wore red and won, so red is lucky" |
| **Appeal to Authority** | "Expert said it, so it's true" | "A celebrity said it, so everyone should do it" |
| **False Dilemma** | Only 2 options exist, but there are more | "Either you love me or you hate me" |
| **Circular Reasoning** | Using conclusion to prove premise | "This is true because I said so" |
| **Appeal to Emotion** | Feelings instead of logic | "This is obviously wrong, everyone agrees!" |
